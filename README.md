## 01 / THE PROBLEM

### Stress is easy to experience. Harder to understand.

Academic pressure, deadlines, uncertainty, and everyday responsibilities can affect how students perceive and manage stress.

Yet self-reflection often stops at a vague feeling of being overwhelmed.

Stress Analyzer introduces a simple, structured process:

- **Measure** — collect ten questionnaire responses.
- **Calculate** — apply reverse scoring where required.
- **Interpret** — calculate a total score and a project-defined category.
- **Visualize** — generate a dashboard showing the score and comparison statistics.
- **Respond** — present practical, non-clinical coping suggestions.

The result is a small but meaningful application of Python programming, data visualization, and behavioral psychology.

## 04 / THE MATHEMATICS

### Reverse scoring

PSS-10 items 4, 5, 7, and 8 are positively phrased and are reverse-scored.

For a response \(x\) between 0 and 4:

\[
x' = 4-x
\]

For example, a response of 1 becomes 3 after reverse scoring.

### Total score

Let \(x_i^*\) represent each response after the appropriate scoring adjustment.

\[
\mathrm{PSS\text{-}10}=\sum_{i=1}^{10}x_i^*
\]

\[
0\leq \mathrm{PSS\text{-}10}\leq40
\]

Higher scores indicate greater perceived stress within the scale's intended interpretation.

### Normalized stress index

For visualization purposes, the score can also be expressed as a percentage of the maximum possible score:

\[
\mathrm{Index}=\frac{\mathrm{PSS\text{-}10}}{40}\times100
\]

**Important:** This percentage is a normalized score index, not a clinical probability or a medical measure of stress severity.

### Group statistics

For a simulated dataset containing \(n\) scores:

\[
\mu=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i
\]

\[
s=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i-\mu)^2}{n-1}}
\]

Here, \(\mu\) represents the sample mean and \(s\) represents the sample standard deviation.

### Worked example

For the response sequence:

`2, 2, 3, 2, 2, 3, 2, 2, 2, 3`

The positive-item responses are all 2, so their reverse-scored values remain 2.

| METRIC | RESULT |
|:--|--:|
| Total score | 23 / 40 |
| Normalized index | 57.5% |
| Illustrative project category | Moderate |

The example demonstrates the arithmetic, not a clinical interpretation.

## 05 / SCORE INTERPRETATION

The project uses the following **illustrative categories** for its demonstration dashboard.

| SCORE | PROJECT CATEGORY |
|:--|:--|
| 0–13 | LOW |
| 14–26 | MODERATE |
| 27–40 | HIGH |

These cutoffs are configurable project choices, not universal diagnostic thresholds. The PSS-10 is a measure of perceived stress, not a diagnostic test, and interpretation should consider the instrument's guidance and context.

### What about the General Adaptation Syndrome?

Hans Selye's General Adaptation Syndrome describes the concepts of alarm, resistance, and exhaustion. These are theoretical stages of a stress response, not categories that can be reliably assigned from a PSS-10 total score.

Stress Analyzer therefore treats the two frameworks separately rather than claiming that a particular score proves a specific physiological stage.

## 10 / ETHICS & LIMITATIONS

Stress Analyzer is an **educational self-awareness project**, not a medical or psychological diagnostic system.

- The questionnaire should use an appropriate, validated version of the PSS-10.
- Results depend on self-reported responses.
- Project-defined score categories must not be presented as universal clinical cutoffs.
- Simulated comparison data is not evidence from real participants.
- Personal responses should not be collected or shared without appropriate privacy protections.
- Anyone experiencing persistent distress or feeling overwhelmed should consider speaking with a qualified mental health professional.
